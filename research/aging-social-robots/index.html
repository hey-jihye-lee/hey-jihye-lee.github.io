Research Interest 01

Older Adults × Social Robots

I am interested in what happens when social robots move beyond the lab and become part of older adults' everyday lives—especially how they fit alongside existing relationships, routines, and support systems.

← all research interests

What I keep coming back to

My early fieldwork showed that distributing the same robot did not lead to the same experience. Some older adults developed relationship-like engagement with the robot, while others treated it primarily as a functional device. Care workers and family members also shaped how the technology was introduced, learned, and sustained.

My current work builds from that observation toward a more differentiated question: if older adults' social relationships and everyday needs differ, how should social robots differ in what they offer, when they intervene, and how they position themselves alongside human relationships?

Questions I'm exploring

  • How do older adults differ in the kinds of relational support they may need?
  • When, in everyday life, might social robot support be useful—or unwanted?
  • How should robots complement rather than replace existing human relationships?
