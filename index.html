Jihye Lee

Senior Researcher at Seoul National University · Ph.D. in Communication

I study how people experience emerging technologies in everyday life, with a particular focus on older adults, social connection, and well-being. My work spans social robots, digital health, and social AI.

I am especially interested in how people's relationships, contexts, and needs differ—and what those differences mean for technologies designed to support them. Across methods and domains, my work asks how technology can meet people where they are rather than treating them as average users.

Older Adults × Social Robots

What happens when social robots enter older adults' homes?

My work follows social robots beyond deployment: how older adults interpret them, form—or do not form—relationships with them, and how care workers, family, and introduction practices shape sustained use.

Geriatric Nursing paper ↓Multi-agent social robots · SNU 2025–2031

Everyday Social Connection

People's relationships differ. Their needs also change within everyday life.

Current studies use network-based clustering, ecological momentary assessment, and longitudinal panel data to understand heterogeneity in social connection and identify where support may be most useful.

Digital Health × Equity

Better average outcomes are not enough if support misses the people who need it.

I use computational approaches to examine how preventive interventions can allocate limited resources while accounting for differences in media use, access, and vulnerability.

Social AI × Identity

When does adapting to people become alignment—and when does it become bias?

This thread examines both sides of identity in human–AI interaction: how artificial agents present themselves, and how models respond differently to the identities of the people they interact with.
