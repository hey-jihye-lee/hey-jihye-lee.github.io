SCIE Q1Journal article

Channel Allocation and Equity in Preventive Campaigns for Older Adults: Agent-Based Modeling Study

Lee, J., Park, J., Kim, Y., & Kong, D.

Journal of Medical Internet Research, 2026

PDFDOI

Background. Preventive campaigns for older adults must decide how to allocate limited resources across media channels. However, these channel allocation and budget decisions rarely use explicit criteria for distributional equity or structured strategic planning tools. Consequently, health systems may optimize average uptake while leaving large gaps across socioeconomic groups and media use profiles.

Objective. This study aimed to develop and apply a data-driven agent-based model as a strategic planning tool for preventive campaigns targeting older adults, comparing channel allocation, personalization, and loss framing options under explicit budget and equity guardrails.

Methods. We built an agent-based model calibrated to national survey data from South Korea on influenza vaccination and routine health screening among older adults (vaccination, N=2405; screening, N=2400). Fifteen prespecified campaign scenarios varied channel allocation across television, digital, and print media; budget intensity; two equity-focused personalization strategies; and graded loss framing. Primary outcomes were final adoption and time to adoption. Equity outcomes included the minimum class-level adoption and the 90–10 gap across latent classes. Each scenario was simulated over 12 monthly steps with 100 Monte Carlo replications.

Results. Personalization improved uptake and equity relative to the integrated baseline. In the vaccination model, adoption increased from 91.2% to 93.3% and 94.6%, minimum class-level adoption rose from 86.8% to 90.3% and 90.9%, and the 90–10 gap narrowed from 5.7 to 4.5 and 4.7 percentage points. In the screening model, adoption increased from 83.8% to 88.2% and 89.5%, minimum class-level adoption rose from 77.6% to 83.2% and 85.3%, and the 90–10 gap narrowed from 9.2 to 7.4 and 6.2 percentage points. Television-only strategies achieved high adoption but had less favorable equity profiles than personalization. Stronger loss framing produced small, monotonic gains in adoption and shortened the time to adoption without worsening equity. Scenario rankings were stable in sensitivity analyses.

Conclusions. Ex ante planning can improve preventive campaign design by comparing channel allocation and personalization options under explicit equity and budget criteria. For campaigns targeting older adults, equity-focused reweighting and class-tailored television–digital portfolios improved or preserved mean adoption while strengthening distributional equity under fixed budgets, whereas undifferentiated channel diversification without personalization offered a less favorable efficiency–equity trade-off.

Designing Artificial Identity: The Identity Design Framework and Research Agenda

Bransky, K., Sweetser, P., Holthaus, P., Laban, G., Winkle, K., Akalin, N., Ashok, A., Lee, J., Khot, R., Bejarano, A., Thijn, J., Moore, R. K., Jang, M., Fischer, J. E., & Lee, M.

Proceedings of the ACM Designing Interactive Systems Conference (DIS '26), Singapore

PDFDOI

The identity design of artificial agents carries growing ethical, psychological, and cultural weight, as ubiquitous language models and diverse robotic forms are blended into everyday use. However, structured approaches to designing coherent and interpretable artificial identities remain limited. To address urgent challenges in artificial identity design, including harmful stereotypes and deceptive practices, we introduce the Identity Design (ID) Framework and an accompanying research agenda. Drawing on emerging work on artificial identity in human–robot interaction and taking an interdisciplinary perspective, we propose twelve design principles across three levels: individual (recognisability, behavioural consistency, identity continuity, memory, persistent goals), group (membership signalling, social alignment, role clarity), and societal (benevolence, artificiality, social justice, transparency). The research agenda outlines open questions around the operationalisation and measurement of identity, social dynamics, and ethical considerations for identity design. Together, they lay the groundwork for future research and responsible practice in robotic, virtual, and multi-embodied agents.

Can Social Robots Reduce the Loneliness of Older Adults? Interaction and Communication between Social Robots and Older Adults

Lee, J., & Joo, C.

Geriatric Nursing, 64, 103359, 2025 · Featured Article

Outstanding Award, Competition for Social Value Enhancement Studies (Center for Social Value Enhancement Studies, 2023), for the research project behind this paper.

PDFDOI

This study explored how older adults living alone interact with social robots over time, focusing on whether such engagement mitigates social and emotional loneliness. Fifteen adults (65 years and older) who had used a specific social robot for at least three months participated in in-depth interviews and observations, analyzed through a constructivist grounded theory lens. Many participants anthropomorphized the robot (e.g., "friend," "granddaughter"), resulting in deeper emotional engagement, especially when two-way communication was sustained. Others used it primarily for functional tasks yet still experienced mild relief from social isolation. Mediators, such as care workers or family members, influenced interaction depth, though older adults with higher digital literacy required less support. Over time, adherence depended on the robot's conversational abilities, accurate initial framing, and user willingness to form a "relationship." Despite these promising outcomes, repetitive dialogue or mismatched expectations led some participants to disengage, underscoring the need to refine design and introduction processes.

Identifying AI Ethical Issues in the Media Content Sector through the Analysis of AI Ethics Policies of Media Content Companies

Joo, C., & Lee, J.

Broadcasting & Communication, 25(3), 5–43, 2024

PDFDOI

The rapid increase in the utilization of AI technology within the media content industry has highlighted various ethical issues associated with its use. This study analyzes the AI ethics policies of media content companies that develop and apply AI technologies to their products and services. By examining the AI ethics policies of 13 companies, we categorized them into 17 keywords. Based on this analysis, we identified four major ethical issues: infringement of intellectual property, spread of misinformation, amplification of bias and discrimination, and replacement of human jobs. This research provides a deeper understanding of the impact of AI technology in the media content field and offers insights for the future formulation of AI ethics policies.

The Impact of Encountering Hate Speech toward Jeolla-do on the Reinforcement of Stereotypes of Jeolla-do and the Use of Hate Speech

Yang, H., & Lee, J.

Locality & Communication, 27(1), 34–65, 2023

PDFDOI

Concerned about the spread of hate speech toward Jeolla-do, this study examined whether encountering hate speech toward Jeolla-do offline and online reinforces negative stereotypes of people from Jeolla-do and prompts the use of hate speech toward Jeolla-do. Surveys were administered to 300 respondents aged 19 or older who were not from Jeolla-do, and the data were analyzed with structural equation modeling. Encountering hate speech offline had a significant effect on negative stereotypes, whereas encountering it online did not. Negative stereotypes in turn significantly predicted both online and offline use of hate speech. Encountering offline hate speech affected offline use, while encountering online hate speech directly affected both online and offline use. Based on these results, the paper discusses the differing characteristics of offline and online hate speech and possible social countermeasures.

A Study on the Perception of Self-Identity of Metaverse Users: Focusing on Symbolic Interactionist Theory

Lee, J., & Joo, C.

Korean Journal of Journalism & Communication Studies, 66(3), 92–138, 2022

PDFDOIKCI

This study clarifies the concept and characteristics of the metaverse and explores self-identity within it through the lens of symbolic interaction theory. Users project their real lives into virtual spaces and sometimes enact aspects of life beyond reality, which raises the question of how users recognize and express identity in the metaverse. Four research questions asked whether users treat the metaverse as a stage on which they construct, modify, and maintain identities; whether they experience it as frontstage or backstage; and whether the metaverse's human communication effects, avatar-mediated interaction, and sandbox freedom support self-expression and impression management.

Most users formed identities in the metaverse, falling into three types: an ideal self, a real self, and a third person. Ideal-self users realized images or behaviors they wished for in reality; real-self users reported little difference from their offline selves; third-person users treated their ZEPETO avatars as objects entirely separate from themselves, as if playing with dolls. Three patterns of stage use (front-and-back, front, back) were identified, and the difference traced to whether users had others with whom they communicated continuously. Users widely reported that verbal and non-verbal communication and the freedom to shape face, body, and style through avatars made self-expression and impression management easier than in other virtual spaces, and some felt able to express a truer self beyond the constraints of reality.

A Study on Strategies of Korean Webtoon Platforms

Lee, J., & Limb, S.

Journal of Arts Management and Policy, 47, 31–63, 2018 · Based on the first author's master's thesis

PDFDOI

As the role and business models of platforms change, many operators have entered the related market, proposing new value to users and searching for business models that maximize profit. This research examines the strategies of Korean webtoon platforms, which created webtoon as a new content type, built their own market, and expanded their influence overseas. Following a literature review, in-depth case studies of three market-leading platforms of different types and origins were conducted to understand the opportunities and motives of each. Strategies were analyzed along three dimensions: content planning and production, approaches to the market, and business model. The results describe commonalities and differences among the three platforms, with particular attention to the development of the freeconomics model, and offer implications for strategy formation in other content platforms.
